O'Bilim: a trilingual, standards-aligned video curriculum

Short video lessons that meet Uzbekistan's national curriculum, build skills for study abroad, and work in three languages.

My role
Co-Founder & Chief Learning Officer: curriculum designer, scriptwriter, production lead, and on-camera presenter
Audience
Students in Grades 5–11 across Uzbekistan, with a focus on girls and families outside major cities
Scope
Curriculum architecture across grade levels and a fully produced Grade 5 pilot unit
Tools
Google Docs and Sheets, Excel, Loom, and a professional video production team

Design phases I led

  • Analyze
  • Design
  • Develop
  • Implement
  • Evaluate

The challenge

In Uzbekistan, girls have had less access to private and digital education than boys, and families outside major cities often have few high-quality options. O'Bilim set out to close that gap with an affordable online program.

The program had to work in a multilingual country, meet national curriculum requirements, and prepare students for study abroad, all through short, engaging video lessons.

My design process

  1. Curriculum architectureI built transdisciplinary units for each grade, each organized around key concepts, a global context, and integrated subjects. The Grade 5 pilot unit, Exploring Identity Through Storytelling, combines language and literature, social studies, and the arts.
  2. Triple standards alignmentI mapped every lesson to Uzbekistan's national curriculum, U.S. Common Core State Standards, and CASEL's social-emotional learning competencies, so the program complemented school studies while building skills for international study.
  3. Designing for videoLessons run as short cycles of video, activity, and reflection. A consistent visual cue (a sand timer) signals reflection pauses, and key vocabulary appears in English, Russian, and Uzbek.
  4. ScriptingI wrote full lesson scripts with scene directions, on-screen text, and a defined color palette, so the production team could build visuals that matched the learning goals.
  5. Production and quality reviewI sourced the production company, presented the lessons on camera, and reviewed every cut with timestamped notes that applied learning-design principles to video.

From my edit notes: text holds still while students read, pauses are long enough to think, visuals build on each other across a lesson, and each step of a math problem stays on screen as the next one appears.

Outcome

I designed the curriculum framework across grade levels and took the Grade 5 pilot unit from concept to finished, professionally produced video lessons. The project secured international grant funding through a competitive proposal.

What I learned

Reviewing the first produced lessons showed me how much visual detail affects learning: moving text, short pauses, and inconsistent cues all add cognitive load. Next time, I would create a visual style and pacing checklist before filming and test a short sample with students before producing full lessons.
