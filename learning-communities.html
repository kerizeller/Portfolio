Student Learning Communities: a multi-phase professional learning program

A program that helps upper-elementary teachers build communities where students feel connected and take ownership of their learning.

My role
Program designer. I designed the program; my KZ Development & Consulting team delivered it.
Audience
School leaders and Grade 3–6 teachers at an international school
Format
Four-phase implementation plan, two-day workshop series, on-site coaching, and a teacher toolkit
Year
2025

Design phases I led

  • Analyze
  • Design
  • Develop
  • Implement
  • Evaluate

The challenge

The school wanted upper-elementary students to feel more connected and take more ownership of their learning. Some elements were already in place, but there was no shared framework. Student groupings tended to stay fixed, and new initiatives risked fading after the first workshop.

My design process

  1. A phased plan, not a one-off workshopFour phases: building a shared vision (months 1–3), a small-scale rollout (months 4–6), full implementation and refinement (months 7–12), and long-term sustainability (year 2 and beyond).
  2. Separate tracks for leaders and teachersLeadership sessions focused on a shared understanding of the model, alignment with the school's strategic plan, and the schedules and structures to support it. Teacher sessions focused on practical frameworks, student agency, and collaborative planning.
  3. A toolkit others could deliverBecause my team would facilitate, I designed materials that carry the method on their own: a Learning Community Design Blueprint, a handout on grouping structures that avoid ability tracking, and a case-study discussion activity.
  4. From workshop to classroomOn-site follow-up was built into the design: practice sessions, classroom observations, and facilitated reflection, so teachers could test the model with real-time support.

Inside the blueprint: community purpose, intended outcomes, student roles, group rotation, weekly rhythms, rituals, and success indicators. Teachers use it to design their own community, so the program doesn't depend on a single facilitator.
